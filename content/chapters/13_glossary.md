# Glossary of Ham Radio and IC-7300 Terms

This glossary explains terms you'll encounter when operating the IC-7300 and in amateur radio in general.

---

## A

**ACC (Accessory Connector)**
13-pin DIN connector on the rear panel for connecting external equipment like TNCs, amplifiers, and audio interfaces.

**ADC (Analog-to-Digital Converter)**
Electronic component that converts analog signals to digital form. The IC-7300 uses a high-speed ADC for direct RF sampling.

**AF (Audio Frequency)**
Audible frequencies, typically 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. The AF knob controls speaker/headphone volume.

**AGC (Automatic Gain Control)**
Circuit that automatically adjusts receiver gain to maintain consistent audio output regardless of signal strength.

**ALC (Automatic Level Control)**
Circuit that prevents transmitter overdrive by limiting audio input. The ALC meter should show minimal movement during normal operation.

**AM (Amplitude Modulation)**
Modulation method where voice is conveyed by varying the amplitude of the carrier wave. Used on broadcast band and some ham frequencies.

**Antenna Tuner (ATU)**
Device that matches antenna impedance to the transmitter's 50Ω output. The IC-7300 has a built-in automatic tuner.

**APRS (Automatic Packet Reporting System)**
Digital communications system for tracking and messaging. Not native to IC-7300 but can interface via external TNC.

---

## B

**Band**
A range of frequencies allocated for specific use. Ham bands include 160m, 80m, 40m, 20m, 15m, 10m, 6m, etc.

**Bandwidth**
The width of a signal or filter passband, measured in Hz or kHz.

**BFO (Beat Frequency Oscillator)**
Oscillator used to make CW and SSB signals audible. In the IC-7300, this function is handled by DSP.

**BK-IN (Break-In)**
CW operating mode where the receiver activates between key elements. Full break-in (QSK) allows hearing between every dit and dah.

**Broadcast Interference (BCI)**
Interference caused by strong broadcast stations overloading a receiver.

---

## C

**CAT (Computer Aided Transceiver)**
System for controlling a radio from a computer. The IC-7300 uses ICOM's CI-V protocol via USB.

**CI-V (ICOM Communication Interface - V)**
ICOM's proprietary computer control protocol used by the IC-7300 and other ICOM radios.

**CQ**
General call inviting any station to respond. "Calling CQ" means seeking contacts.

**CW (Continuous Wave)**
Morse code communication using on/off keying of a carrier wave. One of the oldest and most reliable modes.

**CTCSS (Continuous Tone-Coded Squelch System)**
Sub-audible tone added to FM transmission for repeater access or selective calling.

---

## D

**DATA Mode**
Special mode (USB-D or LSB-D) optimized for digital modes. Uses the USB audio input without voice processing.

**dB (Decibel)**
Logarithmic unit for measuring signal levels. 3 dB = double power, 6 dB = quadruple power.

**DDS (Direct Digital Synthesis)**
Method of generating frequencies digitally. Used in the IC-7300's signal generation.

**Deviation**
In FM, the amount the carrier frequency shifts with modulation. Standard is ±5 kHz for 25 kHz channels.

**Dipole**
Basic antenna consisting of two elements fed at the center. A half-wave dipole is approximately 468/frequency(MHz) feet long.

**DSP (Digital Signal Processing)**
Computer processing of signals for filtering, noise reduction, and demodulation. Core technology of the IC-7300.

**DTCS (Digital Tone Coded Squelch)**
Digital codes used for selective calling on FM, similar to CTCSS but using digital sequences.

**Dummy Load**
Non-radiating 50Ω resistive load used for testing transmitters without creating interference.

**DX**
Long distance communication, or a distant station. "Working DX" means making long-distance contacts.

---

## E

**Echo Back**
CI-V setting that echoes commands back to the computer. Required for most logging and control software.

**EMI (Electromagnetic Interference)**
Unwanted electrical noise that interferes with radio reception.

---

## F

**Filter**
Circuit that passes certain frequencies while rejecting others. The IC-7300 has selectable DSP filters.

**FM (Frequency Modulation)**
Modulation method where voice is conveyed by varying the carrier frequency. Used for local communications and repeaters.

**FPGA (Field Programmable Gate Array)**
Reconfigurable integrated circuit. The IC-7300 uses an FPGA for signal processing.

**Frequency**
The rate at which a signal oscillates, measured in Hertz (Hz). 1 MHz = 1,000,000 Hz.

**FT8**
Popular weak-signal digital mode using 8-FSK modulation. Developed by Joe Taylor, K1JT.

**Full Duplex**
Simultaneous transmit and receive on different frequencies. The IC-7300 is half-duplex (TX or RX, not both).

---

## G

**Gain**
Amplification factor. RF Gain controls receiver sensitivity; Mic Gain controls transmit audio level.

**Ground**
Electrical connection to earth or chassis. Essential for safety and reducing noise.

**Grid Square**
Location identifier using a letter/number code (e.g., FN31). Used in VHF/UHF and digital mode contacts.

---

## H

**Half Duplex**
Communication where you can transmit OR receive, but not simultaneously.

**Hamlib**
Open-source library for controlling amateur radios from software.

**Harmonic**
Integer multiple of a fundamental frequency. Unwanted harmonics must be filtered to avoid interference.

**Heterodyne**
Audible tone caused by two carriers beating against each other. Removed using the notch filter.

**HF (High Frequency)**
3-30 MHz frequency range. Primary operating range of the IC-7300.

---

## I

**IF (Intermediate Frequency)**
Internal frequency used in superheterodyne receivers. The IC-7300 uses direct sampling instead.

**IMD (Intermodulation Distortion)**
Distortion caused by multiple strong signals mixing in a receiver or transmitter.

**Impedance**
Opposition to AC current flow, measured in ohms. Standard amateur impedance is 50Ω.

---

## K

**Keyer**
Electronic device that generates CW code from paddle inputs. The IC-7300 has a built-in keyer.

**kHz (Kilohertz)**
1,000 Hz. Used to express frequency and bandwidth.

---

## L

**LSB (Lower Sideband)**
SSB mode where the transmitted sideband is below the carrier frequency. Convention for frequencies below 10 MHz.

**Linear Amplifier**
Amplifier that increases power while maintaining signal fidelity. Used for higher power operation.

---

## M

**Memory Channel**
Stored frequency, mode, and settings for quick recall.

**MHz (Megahertz)**
1,000,000 Hz. Common unit for radio frequencies.

**MIC Gain**
Microphone input level control. Too high causes distortion; too low causes weak audio.

**Mode**
Type of modulation: SSB, CW, AM, FM, RTTY, etc.

**MOX (Manual Operate Transmit)**
Switching to transmit mode without audio, used for tuning.

**MULTI Knob**
Multifunction control on the IC-7300 for adjusting various parameters depending on context.

---

## N

**NB (Noise Blanker)**
Circuit that eliminates pulse-type interference like ignition noise.

**NR (Noise Reduction)**
DSP function that reduces background noise in receive audio.

**Notch Filter**
Filter that removes a narrow band of frequencies, used to eliminate carriers and heterodynes.

---

## O

**Offset**
Difference between transmit and receive frequencies, used with repeaters.

**OVF (Overflow)**
Indication that the ADC is being overdriven by strong signals. Reduce RF Gain or enable attenuator.

---

## P

**P.AMP (Preamplifier)**
Low-noise amplifier that boosts weak signals before the main receiver stages.

**PBT (Passband Tuning)**
Adjusting the IF filter position to avoid interference.

**PL-259**
Standard coaxial connector used on HF amateur equipment (mates with SO-239).

**PLL (Phase-Locked Loop)**
Circuit for frequency synthesis and stabilization.

**Power Supply**
Device that provides DC power to the radio. The IC-7300 requires 13.8V at up to 23A.

**PTT (Push-To-Talk)**
Switch or signal that activates the transmitter.

**PSK31**
Phase-shift keying digital mode at 31 baud, popular for keyboard-to-keyboard QSOs.

---

## Q

**Q Signals**
Three-letter codes starting with Q used for common phrases. Examples: QRZ (who is calling?), QSO (contact), QTH (location).

**QRM**
Man-made interference from other stations.

**QRN**
Natural noise, typically atmospheric static.

**QRP**
Low power operation, typically 5W or less.

**QSK**
Full break-in CW operation.

**QSO**
A contact or conversation between stations.

---

## R

**RF (Radio Frequency)**
Frequencies used for radio communication, typically above 3 kHz.

**RF Gain**
Control that adjusts receiver sensitivity. Reduce to handle strong signals.

**RIT (Receiver Incremental Tuning)**
Offset receiver frequency from transmit frequency without moving the main dial.

**RTTY (Radioteletype)**
Digital mode using frequency-shift keying, one of the oldest digital modes.

---

## S

**S-Meter**
Signal strength meter, calibrated in S-units (S1-S9, then dB over S9).

**S-Unit**
Unit of signal strength. Each S-unit represents approximately 6 dB.

**Selectivity**
Receiver's ability to separate adjacent signals, determined by filter bandwidth.

**Sensitivity**
Receiver's ability to detect weak signals, measured in microvolts or dBm.

**Sidetone**
Audio feedback of transmitted CW, allowing the operator to hear their sending.

**SO-239**
Chassis-mount UHF connector commonly used for antenna connections (mates with PL-259).

**Split**
Operating with different transmit and receive frequencies.

**SQL (Squelch)**
Circuit that mutes receiver audio when no signal is present.

**SSB (Single Sideband)**
Efficient voice mode that transmits only one sideband. USB above 10 MHz, LSB below.

**SWR (Standing Wave Ratio)**
Measure of impedance match between transmitter and antenna. 1:1 is perfect; 2:1 acceptable.

---

## T

**TNC (Terminal Node Controller)**
Modem for packet radio communication.

**Transceiver**
Combined transmitter and receiver in one unit.

**Transverter**
Device that converts one frequency range to another, used for VHF/UHF/microwave.

**Tuner**
See Antenna Tuner.

---

## U

**USB (Upper Sideband)**
SSB mode where the transmitted sideband is above the carrier frequency. Convention for frequencies above 10 MHz.

**USB (Universal Serial Bus)**
Computer interface. The IC-7300's USB port provides CAT control and audio.

---

## V

**VFO (Variable Frequency Oscillator)**
Tunable oscillator that sets the operating frequency. The IC-7300 has two VFOs (A and B).

**VHF (Very High Frequency)**
30-300 MHz range. 6 meters (50 MHz) is covered by the IC-7300.

**VOX (Voice Operated Transmit)**
Automatic transmit switching triggered by voice input.

---

## W

**Waterfall**
Visual display showing frequency vs time, with signal strength shown as color intensity.

**Watt**
Unit of power. The IC-7300 outputs up to 100W.

**WSJT-X**
Software package for weak-signal digital modes including FT8, FT4, JT65, etc.

---

## X

**XIT (Transmitter Incremental Tuning)**
Offset transmitter frequency from receiver frequency.

---

## Numbers and Abbreviations

**73**
Best regards (used at end of QSO)

**88**
Love and kisses (used between YLs and OMs)

**99**
Go away, leave me alone

**5NN (599)**
Readability 5, Signal 9, Tone 9 - perfect CW signal report

**dBm**
Decibels relative to 1 milliwatt

**kW**
Kilowatt (1000 watts)

**MDS (Minimum Discernible Signal)**
Weakest signal a receiver can detect, measure of sensitivity

**pF**
Picofarad (capacitance unit)

**μV**
Microvolt (one millionth of a volt)

---
